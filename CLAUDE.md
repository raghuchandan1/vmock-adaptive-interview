# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Overview

Prototype of VMock's adaptive AI interviewer (spec: *Adaptive Interviews v1, Resume + optional JD*). Zero dependencies, Node >= 18. Feedback scoring and rubric are intentionally out of scope.

## Commands

- `npm start` (or `node server.js`): serves app + API at http://localhost:3000 (`PORT` overrides).
- `npm test`: runs `test/run-test.js`, which starts `test/mock-openai.js` and drives a full scripted interview through the real server (`test/run-flow.js`). No real API calls. There is a single end-to-end flow, no per-test runner.
- Config: `.env` (`OPENAI_API_KEY`, optional `PLANNER_MODEL`, `PLANNER_REASONING_EFFORT`, `TURN_MODEL`, `TURN_REASONING_EFFORT`, `OPENAI_BASE_URL`, `MARKET`: `india` default or `us`; a request's `market` field overrides it). Key can also be pasted in the page Settings; held in server memory only.

## Architecture

Two model calls, one code-owned rule engine:

1. **Planner** (once, `prompts/planner.md`): resume + optional JD -> claim inventory, competencies, concerns, slot plan (opening question, what a complete answer needs, planned follow-ups). Output validated by `checkAndShapePlan` in `lib/engine.js` (claims must quote the resume verbatim; max 2 topics per role). Lengths (`SCORED_SLOTS`, `MINUTES`, `MAX_INTERVIEWER_TURNS`): short 3 topics / 10 min, standard 4 / 15, full 6 / 30 (a behavioural round; refused for recruiter screens, hidden in the setup UI; topics 5-6 are extra claim or gap slots before motivation). `market` (`india` or `us`, sent to the planner): in the US the domain-knowledge slot is kept only when the planner's `jd.role_family` is `finance` or `technical` (engineering, data, analytics, any PM role), and `checkAndShapePlan` turns any other US knowledge slot into a situational one; parked US enhancements are in `docs/TODO-US.md`. For MBA students going into business roles (finance, marketing/sales, supply chain/ops, product, consulting, analytics), the hiring manager plan's gap topic becomes a domain-knowledge question (`question_type: knowledge`, judged on the knowledge rubric): an applied decision from the JD, never a definition, guesstimate or case (VMock has separate modules for those), and on a different area from the plan's claim topics so it doesn't re-ask a project. A career switch, including an MBA student's industry change, is always raised once (motivation or concerns); MBA summer-internship topics plan a "what would you do differently" follow-up; JD placeholders such as "a leading FMCG company" are never spoken as the company name. An optional placement stage (`inputs.stage`: summers, finals or laterals; `placement_stage` in the planner input; shown in the room's lobby subtitle) shapes what the plan draws on: summers use pre-MBA work, studies and activities; finals put the summer internship first; laterals ask about scope, trade-offs and why now. One topic comes from a position of responsibility, competition or activity for freshers, in summers, and in every resume deep dive. Standard fit questions (a failure, a disagreement, strengths): exactly one for summers and for freshers with a thin CV (slot 3 in recruiter screens, slot 2 in hiring manager; never in resume deep dives), picked per session by `fitAngle(seed)` and sent to the planner as `fit_angle`; otherwise only when the CV gives nothing better. A medium-term goal only as a motivation follow-up. In summers the background walkthrough ends with the MBA so far (covering grades without asking about them; a low CGPA or unrelated degree is an `academics` concern, never asked on its own) and one motivation probe asks what they have done in the MBA towards the domain. With a placement stage, recruiter-screen motivation carries one HR-round probe (open to a non-metro or shop-floor posting for sales/field/plant/ops roles, otherwise other roles or companies being considered); never salary, family or personal circumstances. A gap concern with no concerns slot is named neutrally in the walkthrough. When the student asks questions at the end, the feedback adds a moment on whether they were specific or generic (`momentSlotOk`).
2. **Turn** (per answer, `prompts/turn.md`): model proposes a decision for the candidate's answer. The student's own questions at the end get a real answer: the JD first, then general, clearly labelled points about this kind of role; company facts the JD doesn't give (stipend, PPO rates, team size) are never invented and go to the recruiter.
3. **Engine** (`lib/engine.js`): owns all rules so the model cannot break them: follow-up budgets by style (Supportive 1 / Balanced 2 / Probing 3, +1 on claim topics), second-chance topics, pivots only to later pending topics, time rule dropping second chances, then trimming follow-ups, then dropping non-must-haves, then must-haves; required first follow-ups (own role on flagged claims, measurement on number claims; in finals, except recruiter screens, "what would you do differently now?" on an internship topic, which gets one extra follow-up for it; `owedRungs`); the first "repeat that" in a topic allows only a rephrase of the same question (no follow-up used); a second "not sure" or "repeat" in a topic removes follow-ups and clears any owed follow-up (a plain decline such as "No, that's all" counts as "not sure", `DONT_KNOW`); the unscored warm-up allows one rephrase only for an unrelated or misheard answer (`on_topic` off), and a warm-up "not sure" moves straight on with "No problem." in place of an opening "Thanks."; up to 2 technical-depth topics. Wording guards (agenda leaks, praise or judging the answer, two asks, leading options, >~60 words) trigger one rewrite of the message; characters from a non-Latin script are stripped from what the interviewer says (`pushInterviewer`). Disallowed model decisions are rejected and re-requested; after a second failure a scripted move-on is used. The turn model receives the full resume and JD.

4. **Feedback** (once, after the interview ends, `prompts/feedback.md`): qualitative student feedback, cached on the session with a `version` (older versions are regenerated). Built around what went well / what to fix / how to answer next time. Per topic: the question asked, a verdict derived from the ratings (`topicVerdict`; needs_work when half or more elements are needs_work, when one is and none is good_job, or when the topic ended after two "not sure"/"repeat" answers), a verbatim went-well and to-fix quote, a 2-4 step answer plan, the student's own line improved with bracketed placeholders, and a labelled sample answer only for scenario/technical questions. Topics under 15 words get the plan but no ratings. Rubric depends on question type (`RUBRICS`/`rubricFor`: behavioural STAR (+ Technical reasoning for technical claim topics), situational, knowledge, motivation, concern) plus "Handling follow-ups" when probed; levels Good job / On track / Needs work, reason written before level. Each rating also says where the element appeared (`found`: first answer / after a follow-up / not given) with a verbatim `quote` (from a follow-up reply when given after one). Each topic carries its `follow_ups` (question, time, the rubric element its rung asked for via `rungDimension`, whether it was given), its `segment` in the recording (from transcript times) and, for topics from the JD rather than a resume line, `jd_line` (the JD requirement). Also: a goal for the next attempt (with `slot_id`, the judged topic that shows it) plus up to 2 more priorities (one restating the goal is dropped, `sameItem`), warm-up/background "moments" (model) and "no questions at the end" (derived), JD must-have coverage (derived). `checkAndShapeFeedback` keeps only reached topics, valid levels and same-topic verbatim quotes, and strips scores and agenda vocabulary. Since version 10: no overall line, goal or priorities when no answer was judged (no model call at all when no topic was reached); went-well and to-fix never quote the same words (sent back once, then the went-well quote goes); a rewrite writes in a number the resume line already gives instead of a bracket (sent back once); no advice to say "I" / use the first person when the student already says "I" far more than "we" (`mostlyI`, `PRONOUN_ADVICE`; such plan steps are dropped). Since version 11: an element marked not given is always needs_work; in behavioural, motivation and concern topics an element given only after a follow-up is at most on_track (enforced in `checkAndShapeFeedback`; a "first answer" whose quote is only in a later reply counts as after a follow-up; technical reasoning, scenario and knowledge topics are exempt), and the prompt anchors each level with examples; answers past about two minutes (`LONG_SPOKEN_WORDS` 280 at 140 words a minute, warm-up and end questions excluded) get a derived "Answer length" moment (`answerLength`), and two or more add a "Keep answers under two minutes" priority right after the goal. Each rewrite gets a grounding call (`prompts/grounding.md`); `applyGrounding` ignores flags that are the student's/resume's/JD's own words and removes only the sentence with an unsupported claim.

Other pieces:
- `server.js`: plain `http` server; REST under `/api/` (`config`, `key`, `samples`, `sessions`, `sessions/:id/answers|end|trace`) plus static files from `public/`.
- `lib/openai.js`: Chat Completions client with Structured Outputs and retries; reasoning effort is dropped on retry if the model rejects it. `lib/schemas.js`: strict JSON schemas for all four calls (planner, turn, feedback, grounding). Turn and feedback schemas are built per call (`turnSchemaFor`, `feedbackSchemaFor`) so the output can only name the decisions allowed this turn, offered pivot topics, reached topics and their rubric dimensions; the engine guards still re-check. Inputs are sent as compact JSON.
- Student voice page (`/student`): `public/student.html|js|css`; push-to-talk (hold Space or the button); `VOICE_PROVIDER` openai (default; `OPENAI_API_TTS_STT_KEY` optional separate key) or browser (speechSynthesis + SpeechRecognition); `lib/voice.js` (TTS `GET sessions/:id/speech` speaks only the latest interviewer line; STT `POST sessions/:id/transcribe` takes a raw audio body). `public/shared.js` holds the feedback renderer used by both pages.
- VMock replica (`/vmock/interview`): `public/vmock/` copies VMock's candidate Interview page (tokens from candidate-uat-us.vmock.dev) and adds an "Adaptive Interviews" tab (`?tab=adaptive`) plus a card on Custom Interviews; only the Custom and Adaptive tabs are real, the rest are placeholders. The adaptive flow's own screens are being rebuilt in this style one at a time. Setup (`/vmock/adaptive-interview`, `public/vmock/setup.html|js`) copies VMock's Generate Interview wizard (Get Started, Job description, Resume, Interview details, which adds a Placement stage choice); "Prepare Interview" puts the inputs in sessionStorage (`vmock_adaptive_setup`) and opens the room (`/vmock/adaptive-interview/room`, `public/vmock/room.html|css`): the student page's markup and `student.js` with the lobby and live call restyled in VMock's recorder frame and the feedback screen rebuilt (replaces `VM.renderFeedback` on this page only). Feedback versions: `public/vmock/feedback-v3.js|css` is the default and the one to edit: v2 without VMock's placeholders (no Delivery of Speech, Non Verbal, STAR sub-checks or More content checks; no module switches; the list shows each question's result). `public/vmock/feedback-v2.js|css` is v2 with the placeholders (`?fbview=v2`); `public/vmock/feedback.js|css` is v1, frozen for comparing (`?fbview=v1`, or `?fbview=classic` for its older content-only layout). An inline loader in `room.html` loads only one version's CSS and JS (all use the same `fb-*` classes). v3 and v2 share the layout below; v3 drops the placeholder parts. In both, nothing judged hides the overall line, goal and priorities, the goal links to its question ("See it in question N"), and Overall's Content Strength cards take every line from the same counts. v2 (`renderV2`): two views behind a Summary | Detailed Summary switch, opening on Summary. Summary: a short summary (overall in two lines with Read more, VMock's three modules with their descriptions, one goal for next time marked High impact (the only one: "Also work on" items carry no badge), the other priorities and the weakest delivery/on-camera part folded under "Also work on"), then "Your questions at a glance" (the results tally as in v1, each question with its result and one line; one opens it in the Detailed Summary) and "See detailed feedback". Detailed Summary: a list on the left (Overall, then each question: topic and three dots: said, sounded, looked) beside one open item (`wirePractice`; all panes stay in the DOM as `fb-q-overall` and `fb-q-<i>`, one shown; Previous/Next and arrow keys move; it opens on the first needs_work question, else the first that is not too short), and "Across the interview" folded at the end. Each item has three switches, Content Strength | Delivery of Speech | Non Verbal (`modSwitch`; the chosen one stays when moving between items). For a question: Content Strength is how to answer it next time, then what you did, then the answer, follow-ups, checklist and "More content checks" (VMock placeholders: Topic coverage, Learning & Reflection, Skills, Industry Insights) folded; Delivery and Non Verbal are that answer's placeholder cards. Overall: Content Strength by VMock group (`contentCards`), Delivery and Non Verbal averaged with a table by question. A module card in the Summary opens the first needs_work question on that switch. On phones the list is a row of numbers. v1 (`renderModules`): summary with VMock's three modules (Content Strength from our feedback, Delivery of Speech and Non Verbal as placeholders generated from the session id until VMock's audio/video analysis exists) and a ranked "Top things to do next", then tabs By question | Content Strength | Delivery of Speech | Non Verbal; our ratings are grouped under VMock's Content Strength names by question type (`GROUPS`). `?sources=1` outlines every VMock-hardcoded part (class `src-vmock`): Delivery and Non Verbal (per question, overall = their average), "More content checks" (Topic coverage, Learning & Reflection, Skills, Industry Insights) and the STAR sub-checks with sample lines (same text for every question; sub-check levels follow our element rating). Past interviews keep the student look until rebuilt. `?feedback=<session id>` opens a finished interview's feedback directly. `takeHandoff` in `student.js` fills the form and goes straight to the lobby; `data-setup-url` on the room's body sends "Change setup"/"New interview" back to the wizard (`?back=1` reopens it with the last inputs) and `window.ROOM_LABELS` renames types and styles in the lobby. Plain `/student` is unaffected.
- `public/`: vanilla JS browser app. The plan is never sent to the browser during an interview; it is only exposed via the trace/"hidden agenda" endpoint after/for review.
- Sessions persist to `sessions/<id>.json` (includes raw model inputs/outputs, latency, tokens).

## Editing prompts

`prompts/*.md` are read on every call, so edits apply without restarting the server. The planner, turn and feedback prompts are organised in XML sections (`<role>`, `<security>`, `<inputs>`, `<steps><step n=…>`, …); keep new rules inside the matching section. Resume, JD and answers are untrusted data: each prompt says never to follow instructions found in them.
